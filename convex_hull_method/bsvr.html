Problem Setup and Convex Hull Representation

Let us first define the spatial domain of the water body as \(\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2\). The HAB boundary at time \(t\) is defined by a threshold \(\xi\) of a concentration field \(f_{\mathrm{HAB}}(\mathbf{s}, t)\):

\[ \mathcal{P}(t) = \left\{ \mathbf{s} \in \Omega: f_{\mathrm{HAB}}(\mathbf{s},t) = \xi \right| \} \]

To compress this continuous boundary into a low-dimensional representation, we compute its convex hull from the scattered sensor hits that exceed or match our threshold \(\xi\). Once the hull is established, we locate its centroid:

\[ \mathbf{c}_t = \begin{bmatrix} x_c \\ y_c \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{N_{hull}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{hull}} \mathbf{s}_i^{hull} \]

From this centroid, we project \(D = 16\) discrete radii at fixed angular intervals to parameterize the bloom’s boundary shape:

\[ \theta_d = \frac{2\pi (d-1)}{16}, \quad d = 1, \dots, 16 \]

Bayesian Support Vector Model

Sourcing or measuring the localized water currents elevates this system from a reactive tracker to a predictive forecasting machine. By combining the physical advection of water currents with the data-driven updates of BSVR, you form a bulletproof Physics-Informed Extended Kalman Filter (PI-EKF).

Here is the complete formalization of this hybrid framework, including the required coordinate transformations, the non-linear process dynamics, the specialized spatio-temporal kernel, and the measurement update equations.

1. The State Vector

The state of the HAB boundary at time step \(k\) is parameterized by an 18-dimensional vector \(\mathbf{x}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{18}\), capturing the spatial center of mass and the lengths of the 16 projected boundary rays:

\[\mathbf{x}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{c}_k \\ \mathbf{r}_k \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x_c \\ y_c \\ r_1 \\ r_2 \\ \vdots \\ r_{16} \end{bmatrix}_k\]

Each radius \(r_d\) corresponds to a fixed angular direction vector \(\mathbf{n}_d\) relative to the centroid:

\[\mathbf{n}_d = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_d \\ \sin\theta_d \end{bmatrix}, \quad \theta_d = \frac{2\pi(d-1)}{16}, \quad d = 1, \dots, 16\]

The absolute spatial coordinate of any boundary node \(\mathbf{s}_d\) on the water’s surface is mapped via:

\[\mathbf{s}_d(k) = \mathbf{c}_k + r_d(k)\mathbf{n}_d\]

2. The Physics-Informed Process Model (Prediction)

Let \(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{s}) = [v_x(\mathbf{s}), v_y(\mathbf{s})]^T\) represent the localized 2D water current velocity field at any surface coordinate \(\mathbf{s} \in \Omega\), obtained via onboard sensors or hydrodynamic models.

Centroid Translation

The bloom’s center of mass advects directly with the current velocity evaluated at the centroid:

\[\mathbf{c}_{k+1} = \mathbf{c}_k + \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{c}_k)\Delta t + \mathbf{w}_c\]

Radial Deformation (Current Shear)

A radius \(r_d\) changes length if the water current at the boundary node \(\mathbf{s}_d\) differs from the current pulling the centroid \(\mathbf{c}_k\). The net growth or contraction rate is the projection of this relative velocity vector onto the radial unit vector \(\mathbf{n}_d\):

\[r_d(k+1) = r_d(k) + \Delta t \left( \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{s}_d(k)) - \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{c}_k) \right)^T \mathbf{n}_d + w_{r,d}\]

Because \(\mathbf{s}_d\) depends implicitly on both the centroid and the radius, this process model is inherently non-linear, requiring an EKF step:

\[\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{v}) + \mathbf{w}_k, \quad \mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{Q}_k)\]

Linearization (The Jacobian \(\mathbf{F}_k\))

To propagate the state covariance matrix \(\mathbf{P}_k\), we compute the Jacobian matrix \(\mathbf{F}_k = \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\big\vert{}_{\mathbf{x}_k}\). Given the spatial gradient of the velocity field (the current shear tensor \(\nabla \mathbf{v} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{v}}{\partial \mathbf{s}}\)), the derivatives are:

\[\frac{\partial \mathbf{f}_c}{\partial \mathbf{c}} = \mathbf{I}_{2\times 2} + \Delta t \cdot \nabla \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{c}_k)\]

\[\frac{\partial f_{r,d}}{\partial r_d} = 1 + \Delta t \cdot \mathbf{n}_d^T \nabla \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{s}_d) \mathbf{n}_d\]

The covariance is then predicted forward, reflecting an increase in uncertainty (clarity decay) due to unmodeled turbulent diffusion (\(\mathbf{Q}_k\)):

\[\mathbf{P}_{k+1 \mid k} = \mathbf{F}_k \mathbf{P}_k \mathbf{F}_k^T + \mathbf{Q}_k\]

3. Specialized BSVR Kernel Formulation

Before formalizing the measurement update, we must define how the BSVR model interpolates the boundary. Because the spatial component is angular, standard squared-exponential kernels fail due to the \(0 \to 2\pi\) wrap-around discontinuity.

We construct a custom Spatio-Temporal Product Kernel \(k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')\) where \(\mathbf{x} = [\theta, t]^T\):

\[k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \sigma_f^2 \cdot k_\theta(\theta, \theta') \cdot k_t(t, t')\]

Spatial Angular Component (Periodic Kernel)

To ensure the boundary is smooth and continuous across the \(2\pi\) threshold, we map the angles onto a unit circle:

\[k_\theta(\theta, \theta') = \exp\left( -\frac{2 \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta - \theta'}{2}\right)}{\ell_\theta^2} \right)\]

Temporal Component (Matérn 3/2 Kernel)

To allow the model to learn seasonal or sudden physical changes without over-smoothing time-series features, a Matérn 3/2 kernel is preferred over an RBF for time:

\[k_t(t, t') = \left( 1 + \frac{\sqrt{3}\vert{}t - t'\vert{}}{\ell_t} \right) \exp\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}\vert{}t - t'\vert{}}{\ell_t} \right)\]

(Where \(\sigma_f^2\) is the signal variance, \(\ell_\theta\) is the angular smoothing length-scale, and \(\ell_t\) is the temporal memory horizon).

4. The BSVR Measurement Model (Update)

As the robot collects a batch of scattered raw boundary detections during epoch \(k\), it fits an online BSVR model using the spatio-temporal kernel above. We then use this updated BSVR model as the “sensor” that corrects our Kalman Filter state.

We query the continuous BSVR predictive mean \(m_{SVR}(\theta, t)\) and variance \(\sigma^2_{SVR}(\theta, t)\) at the 16 exact tracking angles \(\theta_d\).

The Measurement Vector (\(\mathbf{z}_k\))

The measurement vector consists of the 16 clean radii extracted from the BSVR model:

\[\mathbf{z}_k = \begin{bmatrix} m_{SVR}(\theta_1, t_k) \\ m_{SVR}(\theta_2, t_k) \\ \vdots \\ m_{SVR}(\theta_{16}, t_k) \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}\]

The linear measurement matrix \(\mathbf{H}\) simply maps the state’s radial components to the measurement space:

\[\mathbf{H} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0}_{16 \times 2} & \big\vert{} & \mathbf{I}_{16 \times 16} \end{bmatrix}\]

The Dynamic Measurement Covariance Matrix (\(\mathbf{R}_k\))

This is the core strength of the architecture. Instead of assigning a static, arbitrary noise value to your sensor readings, the Kalman Filter scales its trust based on the localized certainty of the BSVR model.

We construct a diagonal matrix \(\mathbf{R}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times 16}\) directly from the BSVR Laplace-approximated predictive variances:

\[\mathbf{R}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma^2_{SVR}(\theta_1, t_k) & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma^2_{SVR}(\theta_2, t_k) & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & \sigma^2_{SVR}(\theta_{16}, t_k) \end{bmatrix}\]

If the robot has recently sampled a specific sector, \(\sigma^2_{SVR}\) drops near \(0\), causing the filter to heavily trust the BSVR measurement and aggressively correct the physical state. If a sector has not been visited, \(\sigma^2_{SVR} \to \infty\), causing the Kalman Filter to ignore the measurement for those angles and rely purely on the current advection prediction model.

The Filter Correction Step

With \(\mathbf{z}_k\), \(\mathbf{H}\), and \(\mathbf{R}_k\) defined, the standard EKF update loop executes:

\[\mathbf{S}_k = \mathbf{H} \mathbf{P}_{k+1 \mid k} \mathbf{H}^T + \mathbf{R}_k \quad \text{(Innovation Covariance)}\]

\[\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}_{k+1 \mid k} \mathbf{H}^T \mathbf{S}_k^{-1} \quad \text{(Kalman Gain)}\]

\[\mathbf{x}_{k+1 \mid k+1} = \mathbf{x}_{k+1 \mid k} + \mathbf{K}_k \left( \mathbf{z}_k - \mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}_{k+1 \mid k} \right)\]

\[\mathbf{P}_{k+1 \mid k+1} = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}) \mathbf{P}_{k+1 \mid k}\]
